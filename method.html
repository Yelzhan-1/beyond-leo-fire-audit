Why ISS rules do not carry over

Gravity feeds the flame

Without buoyancy, shutting off fans is the first ISS response. NASA Glenn finds that is unreliable on the Moon and Mars.

Airflow rule on the Moon

Smoke and dust overlap

Station detectors missed some plastics; lunar chamber tests show regolith can mimic smoke in the rover cabin.

False-alarm rule in the rover

Small cabins change tools

A CO₂ extinguisher in a small pressurized rover can exceed safe limits.

    How verdicts are made

    A verdict has four possible values. It is computed by a fixed rule for each row, from the cabin's gravity, pressure, oxygen, volume, dust and distance from Earth orbit. The evidence records then show what was measured, in which conditions, and where in the source.

    1. 1. Cabin conditions. Six cabins: five presets from agency sources (some values flagged as assumed) and one you build.
    2. 2. A written rule. Each of the ten rows has a decision rule, shown below and on every evidence panel.
    3. 3. Evidence records. Records that were measured in matching conditions are listed first, the rest are kept visible as measured under other conditions.

    Where SI fits

    Verdicts are deterministic rules, not model output. The same cabin conditions always give the same verdict, and a test checks every verdict against a snapshot of the original engine.

    • Used today, in preparation. The evidence records and the wording were drafted with an AI model (Claude by Anthropic) from the cited documents, then checked against them. The site and its code were also written with AI assistance.
    • Optional, off unless configured. The search on the evidence page can offer an explanation of the records it found. It sends only those records to an SI service and asks for an answer drawn from them alone. Checking whether it is switched on.
    • Planned. An SI layer that explains and ranks inside the retrieved records, with citations, behind a server-side proxy that holds the key. It will not change a verdict.

    What is calculated

    Three rows are calculated rather than read from a table. All three assume an ideal gas at 23 °C mixed evenly through the cabin. The calculators use the same formulas.

    Limits of the audit

    • These are screening estimates for discussion. They are not a safety certification of any vehicle.
    • Calculations assume an ideal gas at 23 °C mixed evenly through the cabin.
    • The air verdict rescales one Saffire VI fire and treats its far-field sensor reading as the cabin average. Limits are compared in ppm, as the Saffire VI paper does. At reduced cabin pressure the same ppm is a lower partial pressure.
    • The 30 mmHg CO₂ danger level is the NIOSH value of 40,000 ppm at sea-level pressure.
    • Values marked assumed in the table header (the lander and Mars habitat volumes, the Mars atmosphere, sea-level air in the capsule) are design assumptions of this audit. Change them in the cabin builder.
    • Partial-gravity verdicts rest on short ground tests, models and NASA assessments. No long burn has yet been recorded in true lunar or Martian gravity.
    • The flame icons are schematic.
    • Verdict thresholds still come from published summaries in the engine. A small FLEX subset from NASA PSI-69 is committed under data/ for charts only (readme).

    NASA data used

    Loading the list of investigations.

    Saffire IV to VI and the partial-gravity ground tests come from NASA technical reports and the investigators' publications. SoFIE-MIST comes from the investigators' university. The experiments list gives each investigation's records and test conditions.

    The pressurized rover volume (13 m³ of living space) comes from the JAXA and Toyota pressurized rover concept, press release of 12 March 2019.

    FLEX combustion data (PSI-69)

    Processed droplet-combustion measurements from the Flame Extinguishment Experiment, investigation PSI-69 (CC0-1.0, DOI 10.60555/mbq8-0451). The charts illustrate microgravity burning; they do not drive the audit engine. See data/README.md for download URLs and processing.

    Loading FLEX charts

    The CO₂ chart links to the CO₂ extinguisher rule, where FLEX-derived fractions (20% heptane, 30% methanol at 1 atm) set the “breaks” threshold in the audit.
