Calculators

Three numbers a cabin designer can check

The same formulas decide the extinguisher, mask and air verdicts in the audit table. Start from a cabin, then change the volume, pressure or oxygen level to see where a verdict flips.

Empty a CO₂ extinguisher

Respond rule: CO₂ extinguisher

Loading

The ISS unit holds 2.7 kg. Ideal gas at 23 °C, mixed evenly through the cabin. Near the nozzle the level is higher. The FLEX figures are for CO₂ added to air at 1 atm.

Breathe from oxygen masks

Respond rule: oxygen masks

Loading

Litres are counted at 0 °C and 1 atm. The oxygen is assumed to mix evenly and nothing is vented or scrubbed.

Clear the air after a fire

Recover rule: air after the fire

Loading

Scaled from the 1.28 MJ acrylic fire on Saffire VI: 308 ppm of CO in 16.7 m³ at 54.6 kPa. Gas release is assumed to grow in step with heat release. The scrubber airflow and efficiency are yours to set. The prototype flown on Saffire IV and V removed little CO.

Screening estimates, not a certification. All three calculators assume an ideal gas at 23 °C mixed evenly through the cabin. Limits and the Saffire VI reference fire are listed on the method page. To see how these numbers turn into verdicts, open the rover CO₂ cell in the audit table.

Beyond LEO Fire Audit

Ten ISS-era fire-safety rules of thumb, checked against lunar and Martian cabins with NASA microgravity combustion data. Built for the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge, Flame in Freefall, by Team NZE, Almaty.
