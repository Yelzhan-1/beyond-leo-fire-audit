    Empty a CO₂ extinguisher

    The ISS unit holds 2.7 kg. Ideal gas at 23 °C, mixed evenly through the cabin. Near the nozzle the level is higher. The FLEX figures are for CO₂ added to air at 1 atm.

    Breathe from oxygen masks

    Litres are counted at 0 °C and 1 atm. The oxygen is assumed to mix evenly and nothing is vented or scrubbed.

    Clear the air after a fire

    Scaled from the 1.28 MJ acrylic fire on Saffire VI: 308 ppm of CO in 16.7 m³ at 54.6 kPa. Gas release is assumed to grow in step with heat release. The scrubber airflow and efficiency are yours to set. The prototype flown on Saffire IV and V removed little CO.

    Limits of this audit

    • These are screening estimates for discussion. They are not a safety certification of any vehicle.
    • Calculations assume an ideal gas at 23 °C mixed evenly through the cabin.
    • The air verdict rescales one Saffire VI fire and treats its far-field sensor reading as the cabin average. Limits are compared in ppm, as the Saffire VI paper does. At reduced cabin pressure the same ppm is a lower partial pressure.
    • The 30 mmHg CO₂ danger level is the NIOSH value of 40,000 ppm at sea-level pressure.
    • Values marked assumed in the table header are design assumptions of this audit. Change them in the cabin builder.
    • Partial-gravity verdicts rest on short ground tests, models and NASA assessments. No long burn has yet been recorded in true lunar or Martian gravity.
    • The flame icons are schematic.
